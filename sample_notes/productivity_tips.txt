Productivity Tips — Notes

Time Blocking
Time blocking means assigning a specific block of time on your calendar to a
single task or type of task, rather than working from an open-ended to-do
list. Instead of "work on project" all day, you might block 9-11am for deep
work on the project, 11-11:30 for email, and 1-2pm for meetings. This makes
your day's plan visible and reduces the mental cost of deciding what to do
next.

The Two-Minute Rule
If a task takes less than two minutes to complete, do it immediately instead
of adding it to a to-do list. Writing it down, filing it, and later revisiting
it usually costs more time and mental energy than just finishing it on the
spot.

Eat the Frog
"Eating the frog" means doing your hardest or most important task first thing
in the day, before checking email or messages. Willpower and focus are
typically highest earlier in the day, so tackling the most demanding task
first avoids it looming over the rest of your schedule.

Deep Work vs. Shallow Work
Deep work is cognitively demanding, focused effort on a task that pushes your
abilities — like writing, coding, or studying a hard concept. Shallow work is
low-focus, logistical busywork like replying to routine messages. Protecting
blocks of time for deep work, free from notifications, produces
disproportionately more valuable output than the same hours spent
context-switching between shallow tasks.

The Two-List Strategy
Write down your top 25 goals, then circle your top 5. The remaining 20 become
an "avoid at all costs" list — not a someday list. The idea is that
mid-priority goals are often the most dangerous distraction from your true
priorities, because they still feel productive while quietly stealing time
and focus from what matters most.
